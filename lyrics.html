Careful

"I'm so hard on myself."

We say it like a badge of honor. As if pressure never breaks anything, cruelty is a work ethic, and bruises only come from the outside.

But hard on self is self-harm. Not the kind people notice. The quiet kind. The looping thought. The internal verdict. The voice that never lets you land.

Most of us keep unconsciously replacing self-aware with self-harm because we don't know the difference. Self-reflection asks questions. It seeks understanding. Creates room for repair. Leaves you clearer. Steadier. More grounded than you were before.

Self-harm delivers sentences. No curiosity. No grace. Just conclusions paired with deadly phrases like "always," and "never," and "What's wrong with me?"

If it helps you repair, that's reflection. If it punishes you for being human, that's harm. Reflection sounds curious. Harm sounds absolute. Reflection moves you forward. Harm keeps you circling the same moment, over and over again. And if the thought makes you feel smaller, ashamed, that's not growth. That's damage masquerading as accountability.

So ask yourself, does this thought help me, or hurt me? And if you wouldn't say it to someone you're responsible for, a client, or even a younger version of you, it doesn't belong in your head.

The goal is self-reflection, not self-infliction, and internal messages that punish become personal attacks against ourselves.

So let's say it together:

I command my mind to be a safe haven for my thoughts. To grow, to refine, and honor what is true. I vow to be careful with me.
